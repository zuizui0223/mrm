# CREST philosophy-of-ecology literature positioning — 2026-08-17

> **Status:** literature-positioning gate for a future philosophy-of-ecology manuscript. This document does not alter the formal CREST proof ledger and does not assert priority from a negative search.

## 1. Current decision

CREST should **not** be sold as the discovery of contextual representation, ecological identity, ecological model adequacy, intervention-sensitive state definition, task-relevant abstraction, causal abstraction, transferability, or representational-adequacy diagnosis.

The literature gate has become strong enough that the philosophy paper should currently be framed as a **theorem-grounded synthesis / perspective in philosophy of ecology**, unless a later citation-chain audit establishes a narrower priority claim.

The defensible object is:

> Given one proposed coarse equivalence among ecological configurations, CREST separates four scientific adequacy questions—future sufficiency, inherited-semantic coherence, mechanism robustness, and evidential licensing—and maps each failure to a different formal result and remedy.

This **four-contract ecological synthesis** may still be distinctive. Priority is unresolved.

## 2. Novelty claims explicitly excluded

### 2.1 Purpose-, perspective-, and context-relative representation

Pragmatic scientific representation, perspectivism, perspectival realism, model pluralism, and adequacy-for-purpose already establish that scientific representations are evaluated relative to uses, purposes, perspectives, audiences, and standards.

**Do not claim:** CREST discovered context- or purpose-relative representation.

### 2.2 Ecological-system identity

Cumming & Collier, Collier & Cumming, and Delettre directly analyze ecological/complex-system identity, dynamical continuity, boundaries, resilience-linked identity, and multiple kinds of ecological identity.

**Do not claim:** CREST is the first ecological-identity framework or the first multi-axis identity taxonomy.

### 2.3 Ecological model adequacy

Getz et al. explicitly organize ecological model adequacy around state variables, control variables, data determinacy, sensitivity, validity, and coarse graining.

**Do not claim:** ecology lacked a model-adequacy framework before CREST.

### 2.4 Intervention-sensitive ecological states

State-and-Transition Models already classify ecological states through dynamics, thresholds, reversibility, restoration pathways, and management intervention.

**Do not claim:** CREST first made ecological state definitions intervention-sensitive.

### 2.5 Adaptive management under hidden state, model uncertainty, and observation error

MDP/POMDP ecology already combines actions, latent states, observation processes, model/structural uncertainty, belief updating, learning, and policy optimization. Fackler & Pacifici explicitly combine structural and observational uncertainty; Memarzadeh & Boettiger combine model and state uncertainty.

Nicol & Chadès ask *Which States Matter?* and construct state discretizations sufficient to preserve management policies.

**Do not claim:** CREST first integrates actions, model uncertainty, observations, or task-relevant state compression.

### 2.6 Ecological model transferability

Ecological transferability literature already asks when models can be reused across locations, times, and novel conditions and how generalizability fails under environmental novelty, nonstationarity, changed interactions, or data bias.

**Do not claim:** MLTR is the first theory of ecological model transferability.

Safe difference: MLTR fixes one inherited source macro-law and constructs the unique coarsest exact **source-relative repair** under a declared source-target relation.

### 2.7 Predictive states, bisimulation, and state abstraction

Computational mechanics, automata theory, MDP model minimization, bisimulation, predictive state representations, and reinforcement-learning abstraction already define future-/action-preserving equivalences and minimal sufficient states.

**Do not claim:** CCOC/MRM/CED introduce generic minimal predictive states, partition refinement, or behavioral quotient minimality.

### 2.8 Causal abstraction and intervention consistency

Causal-abstraction theory already formalizes exact/strong/approximate mappings between low- and high-level causal models, intervention consistency, information loss, and compositional abstraction error.

**Do not claim:** CREST introduces intervention-consistent causal abstraction or generic micro-macro causal mapping.

### 2.9 Formal representational adequacy, certification, and repair

Recent general work is even closer to the language of the proposed philosophy paper.

- Swanson's *Carriers and Adequacy for Purpose* formalizes bounded operational representations (“carriers”), purpose-relative induced loss, refinement monotonicity, minimal adequate carriers, regime-dependent obsolescence, residue diagnostics, and a revision layer.
- Huang's *Self-Certification of Representation Adequacy* develops static and sequential certification of compressed-representation adequacy, information/task-loss lower bounds, and a boundary example involving kernel switching and representation repair.
- Recent representational-vigilance work also treats representational inadequacy as a diagnostic target distinct from ordinary prediction error, uncertainty, noise, or distribution shift.

**Do not claim:** CREST is the first failure-oriented or formal theory of representation adequacy, certification, obsolescence, or repair.

These recent results materially lower the safe novelty ceiling for the philosophy paper.

## 3. Closest-framework comparison

### Ecological identity versus state-equivalence adequacy

Ecological-identity work asks what constitutes or preserves the identity of an ecological system.

CREST asks a narrower representational question:

```text
identity of an ecological system
    !=
adequacy of treating two configurations as one coarse scientific state
```

Delettre's typological/numerical/global/local identities are kinds or levels of identity; CREST's four axes are scientific adequacy questions about a chosen equivalence.

### Model adequacy versus CREST

Getz-style adequacy evaluates the whole ecological model and its state/control/data/validation structure.

CREST narrows the object to one proposed coarse equivalence and asks why that equivalence fails.

### POMDP/state abstraction versus CREST

POMDPs can encode actions, hidden states, observations, and model uncertainty in one decision system. State-abstraction methods can retain only task-relevant distinctions.

CREST should not deny this integration. Its proposed value is **diagnostic separation**:

> which obligation of the state equivalence failed, and what kind of correction follows?

### Causal abstraction versus CREST

Causal abstraction already owns intervention-consistent coarse models.

CREST's philosophy paper should therefore treat quotient/refinement/abstraction machinery as substrate. Its possible contribution is a cross-contract map showing that an equivalence adequate for one criterion can still face a different scientific adequacy question.

### General representational adequacy versus CREST

Swanson and Huang make it unsafe to claim that formal adequacy, minimal adequate representation, certification, regime change, or repair are new ideas.

The remaining difference is primarily **domain architecture**:

- CREST is ecology-specific;
- it is built from four independently developed theorem programs;
- it distinguishes inherited semantics as its own adequacy contract;
- it separates evidence-licensed reporting from mechanism-robust prediction and future-sufficient representation;
- each axis has a different formal failure output rather than one general task-loss objective.

Whether this difference is philosophically novel enough for a research article remains open. It is already sufficient for a carefully positioned synthesis/perspective article.

## 4. The four-contract synthesis

Take one proposed coarse equivalence

\[
x\sim y.
\]

CREST asks four different questions of that same merge.

### Future sufficiency — CCOC

Can a declared future operation expose a distinction erased by the merge?

Failure output:

> future-word separator / interface lower bound.

### Inherited-semantic coherence — MLTR

After structural replacement, does a previously accepted state meaning remain operationally exact?

Failure output:

> local obstruction / unique coarsest source-relative repair / defect / required history context.

### Mechanism robustness — MRM

Do retained response types agree on the requested future behavior?

Failure output:

> typed or set-valued report / minimal candidate-safe state / discrimination problem.

### Evidential licensing — CED

Has the evidence actually licensed the distinction required by the requested deterministic report?

Failure output:

> ambiguity-retaining report / required target-safe resolution / risk-limited evidence design.

The core synthesis is therefore not “four kinds of ecological identity.” It is:

```text
one proposed state equivalence
        |
        +-- future adequacy?
        +-- inherited-semantic adequacy?
        +-- mechanism-robust adequacy?
        +-- evidence-licensed adequacy?
```

The four answers may be represented together in a larger model, but they remain different scientific questions in the current theorem program.

## 5. Current contribution language

### Reject

> CREST discovers that ecological states are context-relative.

> CREST is the first ecological identity/model adequacy/state abstraction/causal abstraction/representation adequacy framework.

> CREST is the first failure-oriented theory of representation adequacy.

### Safe now

> **CREST is a theorem-grounded synthesis that organizes four distinct adequacy questions about coarse ecological state equivalence: future sufficiency, inherited-semantic coherence, mechanism robustness, and evidential licensing.**

> **Its value is diagnostic: the same coarse state can fail for different reasons, and those failures call for different formal remedies.**

### Priority-sensitive OPEN claim

> CREST **may** instantiate a distinctive ecology-specific four-contract architecture of state-equivalence adequacy.

This is intentionally weaker than “a new theory of representational adequacy.”

Until deeper specialist review is complete, the manuscript should be written to survive even if the word **new** is removed entirely.

## 6. Publication strategy implied by the audit

The philosophy paper should be architected as a **conceptual synthesis / perspective with formal anchors** rather than a priority-seeking grand theory.

Its contribution can remain substantial if it does three things well:

1. makes a hidden ecological modelling commitment explicit — what does one coarse state merge promise to preserve?;
2. separates four adequacy questions that are often encoded together but have different scientific meanings and remedies;
3. connects each question to an existing proved mathematical result without pretending the underlying abstraction machinery is novel.

This strategy is robust to further prior-art discovery.

---

## 7. Remaining literature question

The remaining question is no longer “is CREST new?” in general.

It is:

> **Has philosophy of ecology already presented this same ecology-specific four-contract synthesis—future sufficiency, inherited semantic portability, retained-mechanism robustness, and evidence-licensed reporting—as distinct adequacy questions of one coarse ecological state equivalence?**

A negative answer would strengthen a novelty statement. A positive answer would not destroy the paper; it would shift the contribution further toward theorem-backed clarification and synthesis.
