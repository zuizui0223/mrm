# CREST philosophy paper — journal positioning — 2026-08-17

> **Status:** current submission strategy for the theorem-grounded CREST philosophy-of-ecology manuscript. Journal policies are time-sensitive and must be rechecked immediately before submission.

## Decision

### Primary target — Biology & Philosophy

Why:

- the journal explicitly publishes original work in philosophy of biology across conceptual, theoretical, and methodological issues in the biological sciences;
- it is aimed at readers across sciences and humanities, matching the manuscript's philosophy + mathematical ecology structure;
- recent journal content includes philosophy of ecological model building, ecological extrapolation, conceptual plurality, and abstraction/model questions close to the CREST conversation;
- the manuscript can remain ecology-specific without having to inflate its claim into a general philosophy-of-science novelty thesis.

Current official constraints to design against:

- **article type:** Original Research;
- **review:** double blind;
- **manuscript length:** submission guidelines say **10,000 words or fewer**; aims/scope says articles are usually **above 5,000 and below 15,000 words**;
- **working target:** **5,500–7,500 words**, giving enough philosophical argument to read as an Original Research article while staying safely below the stricter 10,000-word submission limit;
- **abstract:** 150–250 words;
- **current abstract:** 225 words — compliant;
- **keywords:** 4–6 required;
- **references:** name–year; reference list should contain works that are published or accepted; unpublished work belongs in text rather than the reference list;
- **files:** Word preferred; LaTeX allowed for mathematical content;
- **blinding:** author names, affiliations, acknowledgements, funding, and identifying information separated from the review manuscript;
- **LLM policy:** substantive generative LLM use must be documented in a Methods section or suitable alternative section; AI-assisted copyediting alone is exempt, but the current manuscript development exceeds copyediting and therefore requires disclosure.

### Fallback — Philosophy, Theory, and Practice in Biology (PTPBio)

Why:

- direct mission to publish peer-reviewed interdisciplinary work by philosophers of science and theoretically inclined biologists;
- open access and no author fees according to current journal information;
- recent publication of *Ecology in Context* shows an active conversation around ecological context, causal stability, and transferability;
- especially strong fit if the paper remains a theorem-grounded synthesis/perspective rather than a priority-seeking general philosophical theory.

Current caution:

- the official current submission-format page was not reliably retrievable in the audit, so exact word-limit/format requirements must be rechecked before retargeting.

### Reach target — Philosophy of Science

Why not primary now:

- scope fit is real, but the journal explicitly requires a novel contribution to contemporary philosophy of science;
- the current manuscript is intentionally designed to survive the removal of all priority language;
- targeting Philosophy of Science first would create pressure to over-generalize CREST beyond the ecology-specific theorem-grounded synthesis that the literature audit currently supports.

Current official constraints if later retargeted:

- article limit 9,000 words excluding references/online-only appendices;
- abstract 100 words;
- anonymous review;
- substantial AI-generated content must be handled according to the current Cambridge policy at time of submission.

## Biology & Philosophy manuscript design

### 1. Title

Current title is suitable:

> **What Counts as the Same Ecological State? A Contract-Relative Account of State-Representation Adequacy**

It frames a philosophy-of-biology problem without claiming that CREST supplies the first theory of ecological identity.

### 2. Abstract

Current abstract is **225 words**, already inside the required 150–250 range.

Do not lengthen it merely to add theorem details.

### 3. Keywords

Use six:

> **philosophy of ecology; ecological state; scientific representation; model adequacy; causal abstraction; uncertainty**

These cover the philosophical object, ecological domain, closest formal neighbor, and uncertainty layers without inventing a CREST-specific vocabulary burden.

### 4. Main-text target

Target **5,500–7,500 words** excluding reference list for development purposes.

Expansion rule:

- do **not** add theorem families;
- do **not** add more ecological examples;
- add philosophical argument only where the current draft is compressed.

The main missing argumentative mass is one section:

> **Why four adequacy failures should not be collapsed into one adequacy score or one generic abstraction objective.**

This section should distinguish:

1. different starting objects;
2. different quantifier orders;
3. different failure certificates;
4. different remedies;
5. why encoding all four inside one rich decision model does not erase their scientific meanings.

That argument, plus a slightly fuller engagement with ecological identity/model-adequacy literature, should move the paper into the journal's usual Original Research length without sacrificing 雅.

### 5. Double-blind structure

#### Review manuscript

Contains:

- title;
- abstract;
- keywords;
- main text;
- Statements and Declarations that do not identify authors;
- references.

Must not contain:

- author names;
- affiliations;
- identifying acknowledgements;
- identifying funding language;
- self-citations written in an identifying voice.

#### Separate title page

Contains author-controlled metadata:

- title;
- author names;
- affiliations;
- corresponding-author email;
- ORCID(s), if available;
- acknowledgements;
- funding information;
- disclosures as requested by the submission system.

Do not guess any unknown author-controlled metadata.

## LLM disclosure boundary

The current manuscript involved substantive generative assistance during:

- literature triage and positioning;
- proof/claim audit organization;
- drafting and revision of manuscript prose.

This exceeds Springer's definition of undeclared AI-assisted copyediting.

A submission-safe disclosure should therefore be included in a suitable non-Methods section, for example:

> **Use of generative AI.** OpenAI ChatGPT was used during manuscript development to assist with literature triage, organization of claim and citation audits, and drafting and revision of portions of the text. All cited sources, mathematical claims, interpretations, and final wording were reviewed by the human author(s), who take responsibility for the manuscript.

This wording is a working disclosure, not a claim that the tool is an author.

Before submission, recheck the journal's current AI policy and revise the wording if required.
